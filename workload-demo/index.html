Week's Workload — Demo

The weekly workload board for service crews: plan the week, log start and finish times, and watch time on site, man-hours, and money owed calculate themselves — live.

Demo environment: sign up below and you are in instantly — no waiting, no approval. Your demo account and all of your demo data are stored only in this browser on this device.

What it does

• A Monday–Sunday week board of stops: customer, address, start time, finish time, crew size, status, and notes.
• Live calculations, the same way the full workload board runs: time on site per stop, man-hours (time on site × crew), an end-of-day total for every day, and week totals with done / skipped / remaining counts. Finished stops turn green with a ✅.
• A money section that totals what is owed by this month's 1st and owed by next month's 1st from the invoices you enter — automatically.
• Everything saves automatically in your browser, so you can log out, come back later, and pick up right where you left off.

🧪 Reviewer Test Guide — prepared for Andrew Palmer

Andrew — this demo is ready for you to sign up and test hands-on. It is the real workload system in miniature: the same week-board layout, the same inputs, and the same calculations as the production week workload page.

Suggested test flow:

  1. Sign up with the form below — you land immediately in your own working copy, pre-loaded with a sample week of clearly fictional customers so the calculations are visible right away.
  2. Check the math: Monday's first sample stop runs 8:00–9:30 AM with a crew of 2 — that's 1:30 on site and 3:00 man-hours. The day closes with a 🏁 end-of-day total.
  3. Edit a stop — change a finish time or the crew size and save; time on site, man-hours, the end-of-day total, and the week totals all recalculate instantly. Add a stop, mark one skipped, delete one.
  4. Test the money section: add an invoice with a due date, and watch the "Owed by" lines at the top update. Mark it paid and watch it drop off.
  5. Log out and back in — everything you entered is still there.
Known demo limitation, stated plainly: this demo stores accounts and data only in the visitor's own browser (local storage) — there are no server accounts yet, so a demo account lives on the one browser where it was created and does not sync across devices. A production version would move accounts and data to a server; the workflow, inputs, and calculations you see here are the ones that carry over.
